Checked 20 Oct 2026 (IST) on OpenAlex, which indexes Crossref DOIs. Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar were NOT queried directly; check them before submission.

## Verdict
The combined angle appears new: no indexed work applies remote sensing or GIS to Kittur or its satellite forts, and none links the 1824 NAI dispatches to CORONA, the 1924 Survey of India sheets or Sentinel/Landsat data. Rigorous peer-reviewed history of the siege is also sparse. Caveat: Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar and Kannada-language sources were not searched directly.

## Best-fit journals
1. Archaeological Prospection (Wiley): remote-sensing method papers; published the Gujarat CORONA precedent.
2. Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports (Elsevier): applied, case-based desk studies.
3. Remote Sensing (MDPI, open access): fast; publishes many CORONA/heritage papers.
4. Heritage Science (Springer Nature, OA): published the historical-maps + RS work on the Indus.
5. Journal of the Indian Society of Remote Sensing (Springer): regional fit (Honnavar fort precedent), lower impact.
History-led alternative: Indian Economic and Social History Review.

## Novelty check 2 (20 Oct 2026, ~18:40 IST)
Sources: web search (used in place of Google Scholar), Shodhganga, Kannada web (Prajavani, kn.wikipedia, Bookbrahma), Crossref. The Semantic Scholar API returned HTTP 429 (rate-limited) on every try, so it is still unchecked.

### A. CORONA / historical imagery + colonial archives, Indian sites
- Petrie, C.A., Orengo, H.A., et al. (2019). Mapping Archaeology While Mapping an Empire: Using Historical Maps to Reconstruct Ancient Settlement Landscapes in Modern India and Pakistan. Geosciences 9(1):11. https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3263/9/1/11. Uses colonial Survey of India maps plus remote sensing, but for ancient mounds, not forts or battles, and without archival texts.
- Seylon, R. (2009). A Reconstruction of an "Officially Expunged" Kingdom in Colonial South India: Panjalamkurinchi through Memory and Archaeology. Asian Perspectives 48(1). https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/becc9367-b74b-4bac-9ca3-f9bd765cf2d2. The closest conceptual precedent: a poligar fort rebuilt from colonial archives, siege maps and ground mapping, but with no satellite or CORONA imagery.
- Conesa et al. (2014, Archaeological Prospection) is already listed above. It uses CORONA in Gujarat with no archival or military component.
- No published study found that combines CORONA with colonial military archives for any Indian fort, battlefield or heritage site.

### B. Remote sensing of Kittur fort or old Sangolli
- None found for the Kittur fort, its citadel, or the submerged old Sangolli.
- Old Sangolli: The Hindu (2015), "Banyan tree testimony to Rayanna's hanging", https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/banyan-tree-testimony-to-rayannas-hanging/article7440944.ece, reports that the original Sangolli was submerged by the Malaprabha irrigation project and the residents moved to a new Sangolli. The Malaprabha (Renuka Sagara / Navilutheertha) dam was completed c.1972-74. This newspaper claim must be checked against the 1924 Survey of India sheet and CORONA frames: if the old village falls inside the reservoir footprint, pre-dam CORONA (1960s) is a strong, unique dataset. Note that present-day Sangolli lies in Bailhongal taluk; confirm the geometry before relying on this.

### C. Prior identification of the 'lower fort / citadel' layout
- No published plan or analysis of a Kittur lower-fort/citadel layout was found. Sources only describe a ~23-acre basalt fort with moat, palace, gateways and tanks (Incredible India; Wikipedia).
- The Hindu (9 Oct 2024) quotes Karnataka's Revenue Minister saying reconstruction was "delayed due to non-availability of the original designs": https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/kittur-fort-to-be-developed-at-a-cost-of-30-crore-by-government-of-karnataka/article68733159.ece. This is useful framing for the paper.

### D. ASI / Karnataka Dept of Archaeology
- No published excavation report or layout survey was found online. The fort is a state-protected site managed by the Dept of Archaeology, Museums and Heritage; its museum opened in 1967.
- Prajavani (25 Oct 2025), "Kittur Excavation Neglect" (Kannada): https://www.prajavani.net/district/belagavi/kittur-history-excavation-neglect-cm-3598151. Reports that the government has not taken up excavation; researchers call for it.
- Prajavani (14 May 2026): an archaeology engineer was served notice over cement plaster on the palace walls. https://www.prajavani.net/district/belagavi/kittur-palace-renovation-archaeology-engineer-notice-3982650
- A 2011 blog (karnatakatravel.blogspot.com) photographed trial pits in the palace courtyard. Any departmental report on these is unpublished or not online; ask the Dept / Indian Archaeology – A Review volumes.

### E. Theses and books (Shodhganga, Kannada)

### Verdict: is "first in India" defensible?
Not as a flat claim. Coverage of Kannada print, Indian Archaeology – A Review, and the unpublished departmental files is incomplete, and Petrie et al. (2019) and Seylon (2009) are partial precedents. A qualified, specific claim is defensible:

"To our knowledge, this is the first study to integrate declassified CORONA imagery with contemporary colonial military records to reconstruct an Indian fort and its siege landscape, and the first remote-sensing study of Kittur Fort and the 1824 Kittur campaign."

Safer variant: "...the first to combine colonial siege correspondence, early Survey of India mapping, CORONA and Sentinel/Landsat data for a South Asian fortification." Avoid "first ever" and avoid "in India" without "to our knowledge".
