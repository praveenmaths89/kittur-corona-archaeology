### p.11 (Deacon to Pierce, Camp before Kittoor, 8 Dec 1824)
"...a copy of a letter from Major Trewman, who Commanded the most advanced Post, nearest to the East Gate [OCR: 'Fect Gate'; probably "East"/"Fort Gate" – check image] of Kittoor and was [OCR garbled] during the latter part of the Siege, containing an account of some events not detailed in my original dispatch, which however make some difference as to the mode in which the Fort of Kittoor came into our possession..."
### p.12 (Maj. J. J. Trewman, Advanced Post, Camp before Kittoor, 8 Dec 1824)
"Having heard a report that the Fort of Kittoor had surrendered by Capitulation, I take the liberty to submit... that I advanced repeatedly close to the Crest of the Glacis, and according to the instructions of Lt Colonel Deacon C.B. Summoned the Enemy to Surrender the Fort, and to open the Gates that the Troops might take possession... they refused repeatedly to open more than the Wicket for the admission of myself alone as they had done once before early in the Morning, when, receiving a Note from Colonel [p.13:] Deacon to be guarded against Treachery, I effected my retreat out of the Fort."
### p.13
"In reply to my last summons and threat to blow open the Gates by a Fire from my [19?]th Battery [OCR 'my 19the Battery'; probably '9-pounder'/'Battery'] close within 12 [yards? OCR 'within 12 over and a their new'] they again decidedly refused to surrender. — The Defences Round the Gates were at that time crowded with Armed Men, and I had my Guns loaded with Canister and pointed at them ready to open, when they disappeared, and in a few Moments the Mullaabad [Malabar? OCR 'Mallahzabad'] Light Infantry appeared on the defences of the outer [fort/work?] [OCR: 'enry'] had several Guns mounted to enfilade the principal Street which I occupied [OCR garbled] where the gate. Colonel Conry [Cony?] with his Corps, that entered the Works by escalade between the lower, and the Upper Fort and penetrating to the Gates, opened the Wicket from the Interior, & let me in..."
### p.14
"The Keys of the Gates were not delivered up till we were in Possession of the Palace. I hope I may be excused the liberty I have taken in making this Report... (Signed) J. J. Trewman, Major Com[manding] Advanced Post."
### pp.3-6 (context, no topography): Committee on treasure "in the Palace" (Kittoor Palace, 6 Dec 1824); Deacon dated "Camp before Kittoor December 6th 1824"; Pierce (p.6): "the Fort of Kittoor [having] surrendered by capitulation and not been captured by assault"; Palace attempted to be burnt night of 5th Dec.
Topographic inferences: separate lower (outer) fort and upper fort with escalade between them; gates with wicket; glacis crest; an "advanced post" nearest the (East?) gate; a battery close to the gate; principal street enfiladed from outer works; palace inside.
